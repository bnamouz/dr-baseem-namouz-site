Who is allowed to diagnose ADHD in Israel?

Dr. Baseem Namouz, specialist pediatrician

In brief: In Israel, an ADHD diagnosis may be made only by physicians who have specialized and gained experience in the field, and by specialist psychologists who have specialized in it. This is set out in Israeli Ministry of Health Medical Division Circular 15/2021 (in Hebrew), issued in December 2021. A teacher, school counselor or didactic assessor can raise a suspicion and provide important information, but they do not make a diagnosis.

Who diagnoses children and adolescents

According to the circular, the following may diagnose children:

  • A physician who has specialized and gained experience in treating ADHD, including a specialist in pediatric neurology and child development, a specialist in child and adolescent psychiatry, and a pediatrician with at least three years of experience in child development (a developmental physician).
  • A specialist pediatrician or specialist family physician who has completed a recognized Ministry of Health training course and gained experience in the field of ADHD. These physicians diagnose children aged 6 and over only.
  • A specialist psychologist who has specialized and gained experience in treating ADHD.

Who diagnoses adults

  • A physician who has specialized and gained experience in treating ADHD, including a specialist in adult neurology or adult psychiatry.
  • A specialist family physician who has completed a recognized Ministry of Health training course and gained experience in the field.
  • A specialist psychologist who has specialized and gained experience in treating ADHD.

The role of the psychologist and the role of the physician

A specialist psychologist may diagnose. However, the circular states that a diagnosing psychologist will refer the person assessed to a physician, as relevant and as needed, to look into co-occurring conditions and to complete the evaluation of whether medication is needed.

Who does not make a diagnosis

Social workers, didactic assessors, occupational therapists, physiotherapists and speech and language therapists can assist the diagnosing clinician, at their discretion. Under the circular, responsibility for the diagnosis rests solely with the diagnosing physician or psychologist.

Two things that are often confused:

  • A psycho-didactic assessment is intended mainly for learning disabilities. The Ministry of Health explains (in Hebrew) that it can also provide information about attention symptoms, but that is not its main purpose.
  • A computerized test is not a diagnosis. According to the Ministry of Health, tests such as MOXO and TOVA are neither necessary nor sufficient for making a diagnosis. They are a supporting tool that helps the physician be more precise, and the clinical interview is always the most important tool.

What an assessment must include

The circular states that the assessment should take a reasonable amount of time, 45 to 60 minutes. For children and adolescents it must include:

  • A detailed history of the child and the family, including heart conditions.
  • A physical, neurological and developmental examination, appropriate to age.
  • A full evaluation against the criteria of the current edition of the DSM.
  • An evaluation of other possible disorders.
  • Validated diagnostic questionnaires for parents and teachers.

As needed, the clinician will recommend additional tools: a computerized test, psychological tests, a psychiatric evaluation, a cognitive evaluation or an assessment of learning skills. These are not required in every assessment.

For adults, the assessment includes a detailed history, covering developmental background and childhood symptoms, an evaluation against the DSM, an evaluation of other disorders, a physical examination as needed, and questionnaires adapted for adults.

How to check that the clinician is qualified

  1. License and specialist title. In the Ministry of Health register of license holders (in Hebrew) you can search by name or license number and see the field of specialization.
  2. Pediatrician or family physician. The Ministry of Health publishes a list of physicians who have completed the training and given notice that they have opened a clinic (in Hebrew). A physician who has completed the training but has not given notice of opening a clinic will not appear on it, so you can also ask the physician directly.
  3. Ask directly. You are entitled, and encouraged, to ask the clinician about their training in the field, how long they have worked in it and what their assessment includes.

Frequently asked questions

From what age can a child be diagnosed?

A specialist pediatrician or specialist family physician who has completed the training diagnoses from age 6. Below age 6, diagnosis is made by a pediatric neurologist, a child and adolescent psychiatrist or a developmental physician. According to the Ministry of Health, diagnosis is usually made at primary school age; diagnosis at kindergarten age is possible when symptoms are pronounced, but it is not common.

Can the teacher or school counselor diagnose?

No. They can draw the parents' attention to a concern and give the clinician information about functioning in class, for example in a teacher questionnaire.

Does the health fund pay for the assessment?

According to Kol-Zchut (in Hebrew), health funds pay for a medical ADHD assessment for children and young people up to age 18. Computerized attention tests are not included in the health basket, and supplementary insurance plans subsidize them with a co-payment.

Is a psychologist's diagnosis enough to receive medication?

The need for medication is evaluated by a physician. A diagnosing psychologist refers to a physician for this purpose.
